$A$ took the numbers $x_1, \ldots, x_5$, found their pairwise sums $a_1, \ldots, a_{10}$, and challenged $B$ to reconstruct $x_1, \ldots x_5$ from $a_1, \ldots, a_{10}$. Can $B$ succeed?

\emph{Remark}: For $n = 2^k$ this is not always possible. For instance, the quadruples $(0, 1, 2, 4)$ and $(-0.5, 1.5, 2.5, 3.5)$ give the same sixtuple $(1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6)$.
