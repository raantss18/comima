Let $E$ be a finite set of points in 3-space with the following properties:

(a) $E$ is not coplanar. (b) No three points of $E$ are collinear.

\emph{Prove}: Either there are five points in $E$, which are vertices of a convex pyramid the interior of which is free of points of $E$, or there exists a plane, which contains exactly three points of $E$.
