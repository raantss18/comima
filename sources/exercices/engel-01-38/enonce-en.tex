The integers $1, \ldots, n$ are arranged in order. In one step you may take any four integers and interchange the first with the fourth and the second with the third. Prove that, if $n(n - 1)/2$ is even, then by means of such steps you may reach the arrangement $n, n - 1, \ldots, 1$. But if $n(n - 1)/2$ is odd, you cannot reach this arrangement.
