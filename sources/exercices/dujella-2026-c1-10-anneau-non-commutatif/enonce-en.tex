\textbf{Problem 1.10 (Dujella, 2026).} Define an analogue of a $D(n)$-$m$-tuple in a non-commutative ring and prove some non-trivial results concerning the existence of $D(n)$-quadruples, e.g. in the ring of $2\times 2$ matrices with integer entries.

\medskip

\textit{Remark.} Dujella and Franušić (arXiv:2604.23404) proposed considering the Jordan product $A \circ B = \frac{1}{2}(AB+BA)$. The notion of regular triples naturally generalizes in this situation: if $A \circ B = R^2$, then $C = A+B+2R$ satisfies $A \circ C = (A+R)^2$ and $B \circ C = (B+R)^2$.
