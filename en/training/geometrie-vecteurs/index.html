Vectors, midpoints and similar triangles

  • In coordinates, $\overrightarrow{AB} = B - A$: subtract the coordinates. Chasles' relation: $\overrightarrow{AB} + \overrightarrow{BC} = \overrightarrow{AC}$.
  • Dot product: $\vec u \cdot \vec v = \|\vec u\|\,\|\vec v\| \cos\theta$, and in coordinates $xx' + yy'$. It is zero if and only if the vectors are orthogonal. Expand: $\|\vec u \pm \vec v\|^2 = \|\vec u\|^2 \pm 2\,\vec u\cdot\vec v + \|\vec v\|^2$.
  • Midpoint of $[AB]$: $\frac{A+B}{2}$. Centroid of $A_1, \ldots, A_n$: $\frac{A_1+\cdots+A_n}{n}$.
  • $ABCD$ is a parallelogram if and only if $\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{DC}$, that is $A + C = B + D$: the diagonals have the same midpoint.
  • If the origin is the circumcentre $O$, the orthocentre is $H = A + B + C$.
  • Two triangles are similar if they have the same angles, or proportional sides. For a ratio $k$, lengths are multiplied by $k$ and areas by $k^2$.
