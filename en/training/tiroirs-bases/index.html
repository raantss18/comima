The pigeonhole principle

Objects are put into drawers; each object goes into exactly one drawer. If $n + 1$ objects are placed in $n$ drawers ($n \ge 1$), some drawer holds at least two objects.

General form: if $k \ge 1$ is an integer and $kn + 1$ objects are placed in $n$ drawers, some drawer holds at least $k + 1$ objects.

Why: if every drawer held at most $k$ objects, there would be at most $kn$ objects in all.

The hard part is always choosing the drawers (for example: remainders on division by $m$, months, small squares).
