Infinite descent and arithmetic invariants

Infinite descent assumes a smallest positive integer solution, then constructs a strictly smaller one. The construction must preserve integrality, the equation and positivity; a positive integer cannot decrease forever.

Congruences can force all variables to share a divisor. Homogeneity then allows division and repetition. Fourth powers are $0$ or $1$ modulo $16$; a cube divisible by $3$ has a base divisible by $3$.

For $x^2+xy-y^2=1$, any common divisor of $x,y$ divides the left side, hence $1$. The map $(x,y)\mapsto(x+y,x+2y)$ preserves the equation; its inverse $(2x-y,y-x)$ is usable only when both coordinates stay positive.
