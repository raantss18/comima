Winning and losing positions

In a finite game without chance, a position is winning for the player to move if some legal move reaches a losing position. It is losing if every move reaches a winning one. With no legal move, the player loses unless explicitly stated otherwise.

In Wythoff, remove a positive number from one pile, or the same positive number from both; taking the last token wins. Total size decreases, so classify positions starting at $(0,0)$.

Computed lists suggest conjectures. A strategy requires two proofs: no move connects two claimed losing positions, and every other position can reach one. In a game with memory, the previous removal belongs in the state.
