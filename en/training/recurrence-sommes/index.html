Sums and induction

To prove that a statement $P(n)$ (depending on an integer $n$) holds for every integer $n \ge 1$:

  1. Base case: check that $P(1)$ is true.
  2. Inductive step: assume that $P(n)$ is true for an arbitrary integer $n \ge 1$ and deduce that $P(n+1)$ is true.

We conclude that $P(n)$ holds for every integer $n \ge 1$. Both steps are necessary. Here $1 + 2 + \dots + n$ is the sum of the integers from $1$ to $n$.

Key example: $1 + 2 + \dots + n = \dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}$.
