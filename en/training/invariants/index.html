Invariants

When a process changes a situation step by step (one move at a time), look for a quantity that does not change at each move: an invariant. Examples: a parity, a remainder modulo $m$, a (possibly weighted) sum, a product, a GCD, the difference between the numbers of black and white squares of a colouring.

Rule. If the invariant has different values in the starting state and in the target state, the target state is impossible to reach.

Warning. The converse is false in general: two states with the same value of the invariant are not necessarily connected. To show that a state can be reached, you must give a sequence of moves that leads to it.

A monovariant is a quantity that strictly increases at every move, or strictly decreases at every move. If it is a non-negative integer that strictly decreases, it drops by at least $1$ each time: the process stops after finitely many moves.
