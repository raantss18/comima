Factorization and symmetry

Expanding verifies an identity; factorization reveals zeros. Use $u^3+v^3=(u+v)(u^2-uv+v^2)$ and the difference of squares.

A symmetric polynomial is unchanged by exchanging variables; an antisymmetric one changes sign. If $F(x,x)=0$, a factor $x-y$ appears. Over the reals antisymmetry gives this vanishing. Dividing by $x-y$ changes antisymmetry into symmetry and conversely.

For $x^4+x^2+1$, add and subtract $x^2$: $(x^2+1)^2-x^2$. For $A(x)A(-x)$, parity explains why odd powers disappear.
