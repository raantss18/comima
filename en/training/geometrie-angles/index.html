Angles, triangles and circles

  • The angles of a triangle add up to $180^\circ$; those of a convex $n$-gon ($n \geq 3$) to $(n-2) \times 180^\circ$.
  • Inscribed angle (vertex on the circle): half the central angle subtending the same arc. So two inscribed angles subtending the same arc are equal.
  • Pythagoras: in a right triangle, $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ ($c$ the hypotenuse, $a$ and $b$ the legs).
  • Euler line: in a non-equilateral triangle, the centroid $G$, orthocentre $H$ and circumcentre $O$ are collinear, with $\vec{OH} = 3\vec{OG}$ (in an equilateral triangle they coincide).
