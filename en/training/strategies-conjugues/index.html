Conjugates, rationalization and floors

The conjugate of $u+v\sqrt d$ is $u-v\sqrt d$. Their product is $u^2-dv^2$, an integer for integer $u,v,d$. Conjugating a power changes the sign of the $\sqrt d$ coefficient and preserves the rational coefficient.

Rationalize $\sqrt{n^2+1}-n$ by multiplying by the conjugate to get $1/(\sqrt{n^2+1}+n)$, avoiding subtraction of two large nearby values.

The floor $\lfloor x\rfloor$ is the greatest integer at most $x$. If $A$ is an integer and $0<t<1$, then $\lfloor A-t\rfloor=A-1$.

Counting pairs $(d,k)$ with $dk\le N$ gives $\sum_{n=1}^N\tau(n)=\sum_{d=1}^N\lfloor N/d\rfloor$, where $\tau$ counts positive divisors. Weight each pair by $d$ to obtain the analogous divisor-sum formula.
