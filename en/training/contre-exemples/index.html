Examples and counterexamples

A universal statement claims that a property $P(n)$ holds for every $n$ in a set $E$.

A counterexample is an $n_0$ in $E$ for which $P(n_0)$ is false.

If $E$ is infinite, checking $P(n)$ on many examples does not prove the statement: a proof valid for every $n$ in $E$ is needed.

On the other hand, a single counterexample is enough to show that a “for every $n$” statement is false.

The negation of “for every $n$ in $E$, $P(n)$” is “there exists $n$ in $E$ such that not $P(n)$”.

If $E$ is finite, checking every case one by one is a proof.

For an existential statement (“there exists $n$ in $E$ such that $P(n)$”), a single example is enough to prove it.
